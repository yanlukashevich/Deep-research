## Answer

Current scientific consensus is that Mercury has no confirmed natural satellites [1][2]. 🟡

According to authoritative sources Mercury has zero moons, so there are no names to list [1][2]. 🟡

In 1974 the Mariner 10 spacecraft detected an ultraviolet signal that was briefly interpreted as a possible moon, but the signal was later traced to the distant star 31 Crateris, disproving the claim [2]. 🔴

Mercury’s lack of moons is explained by its small mass and extreme proximity to the Sun, which produce strong solar tidal forces, high solar radiation, a very small Hill sphere, and dynamical instabilities that make any captured or formed satellite unstable over long periods [1][2]. 🟡
